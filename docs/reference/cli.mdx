### piloop agent

Act on one agent.

#### piloop agent messages

```text
piloop agent messages [ARGS]
```

Take the messages that wait for this agent's turn, and print them.

A hook of the harness runs it during a turn, so a message reaches the turn in progress. It prints nothing when no
message waits.

**Parameters**:

* `HARNESS, --harness`: The harness whose hook reads them: one message a line without it.

#### piloop agent cancel

```text
piloop agent cancel [OPTIONS] AGENT_SESSION_ID
```

Cancel an agent of any role. The daemon acts at once.

A requested agent never starts. A running one is ended with every process that it started, at once or, with
`--wrap-up`, after it was told to save its work and stop. For an implementer, this cancels its run (`piloop run cancel`).

**Arguments**:

* `AGENT_SESSION_ID`: The id of the agent. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--wrap-up`: Tell the agent to save its work and stop first, and end it 10 minutes later.

### piloop attach

```text
piloop attach AGENT_SESSION_ID
```

Print the last lines that an agent wrote, running or ended: `[agents] attach_lines` of them.

An agent is a background process, with no terminal to type into: `piloop prompt` sends it a message.
A running agent's lines come from the latest snapshot of its session, on any machine: a few seconds old, 30 at most.

**Arguments**:

* `AGENT_SESSION_ID`: The id of the agent. **[required]**

### piloop campaign

Act on a campaign as a whole.

#### piloop campaign parent

```text
piloop campaign parent [OPTIONS] REF [ARGS]
```

Put a campaign under another, or make it a root with --clear.

A campaign has at most one parent, in the same piloop home, and never itself or one of its descendants.
`piloop campaign tree` shows the tree.

**Arguments**:

* `REF`: The campaign name, its id, or a path inside its repo. **[required]**
* `PARENT_REF`: The campaign to put it under.

**Parameters**:

* `--clear`: Make the campaign a root.

#### piloop campaign tree

```text
piloop campaign tree
```

Print every campaign under its parent, with its type and its status.

#### piloop campaign stop

```text
piloop campaign stop [OPTIONS] REF
```

Stop a campaign. The daemon stops it at once, as when a `[stop]` condition holds.

This works also while the campaign is paused or held by its stop rule. It drops the queued runs and the requested
agents. Each running implementer is told to save its work and stop, and is ended 10 minutes later. What the
implementers saved is scored.
With `--kill`, the implementers are ended at once, and what they committed is still scored. On a stopped campaign,
`--kill` ends the implementers that are still winding down. The campaign's `piloop.toml` is not changed.

**Arguments**:

* `REF`: The campaign name, its id, or a path inside its repo. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--kill`: End the running implementers at once, without the wrap-up prompt.
* `--reason STR`: Why, for `stopped_reason`. *[default: ""]*

#### piloop campaign fork

```text
piloop campaign fork [OPTIONS] REF
```

Make a new campaign that starts from a version of another one, by default its best, in a clone of its checkout.

The source may be in any state, retired too, and goes on as it is. The fork takes its stored config and its repo,
so it sees the ideas of every campaign on the repo. It starts with no ideas of its own and an empty workspace.
The daemon measures its starting version as its baseline.

**Arguments**:

* `REF`: The campaign name, its id, or a path inside its repo. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--at STR`: The version to start from, as the source's vcs names one. Default: its best.
* `--name STR`: The new campaign's name. Default: \<source\>-\<n\>.

#### piloop campaign sync

```text
piloop campaign sync [OPTIONS] REF
```

Read a campaign's piloop.toml again. A campaign that the server cloned first takes its repository's new versions.

Its checkout moves forward to them; its candidates and their scores stay. New code comes in with
`piloop candidate import`, or a fork with `piloop campaign fork --at`. A local checkout is read where it is:
`piloop init <path>` does the same. For a private repository: `--secret`, or a token piped in with
`--token-stdin`.

**Arguments**:

* `REF`: The campaign name, its id, or a path inside its repo. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--secret STR`: The name of the server's secret that the host of the campaign's repository asks for.
* `--token-stdin`: Read a token that the repository's host accepts from stdin, for this sync alone: nothing keeps it.

#### piloop campaign reopen

```text
piloop campaign reopen --reason STR REF
```

Set a stopped campaign active again, once it is closed and its runs are over.

The runs, hours and tokens that its stop rule limits count again from now, so it gets each of them again. A rule
that would stop it at once, a target that it reached say, refuses: change the rule in piloop.toml and run
`piloop init` instead. A retired campaign does not reopen: fork it.

**Arguments**:

* `REF`: The campaign name, its id, or a path inside its repo. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--reason STR`: Why, with ids. **[required]**

#### piloop campaign retire

```text
piloop campaign retire --reason STR REF
```

Retire a stopped campaign for good, once it is closed and its runs are over.

It changes no more, `piloop status` leaves it out unless `--status retired` asks for it, and a new campaign may
take its name. Its rows, events and archived files stay, and its id still names it. The reason goes into a note on
the campaign.

**Arguments**:

* `REF`: The campaign name, its id, or a path inside its repo. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--reason STR`: Why, with ids. **[required]**

### piloop candidate

Show a candidate, import a starting point, measure a candidate again, note one, or exclude one from the best.

#### piloop candidate show

```text
piloop candidate show CANDIDATE_ID
```

Print a candidate: where it stands, its metrics beside its parent's, and each rung of the ladder that it measured.

The metrics are the means under the campaign's current evaluator. A rung's time is its own, without the rungs
before it.

**Arguments**:

* `CANDIDATE_ID`: The candidate. **[required]**

#### piloop candidate import

```text
piloop candidate import [OPTIONS] REF VERSION
```

Register the files of a version as a reference candidate: a starting point for a run.

The daemon scores it into the pool with the current evaluator, as any candidate: it may become the best. To import
a candidate of another campaign, bring its `revision` into this campaign's repo first.

**Arguments**:

* `REF`: The campaign name, its id, or a path inside its repo. **[required]**
* `VERSION`: A version in the campaign's repo, as its vcs names one: a revision, a branch, a tag. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--summary STR`: A summary of the candidate. Default: the version's own summary.

#### piloop candidate remeasure

```text
piloop candidate remeasure [OPTIONS] CANDIDATE_ID
```

Ask the daemon to evaluate a candidate `--repeats` more times, with the current evaluator.

Use it when a fault of the host spoiled a measurement (a full disk, a lost GPU), or when load made it noisy. The
candidate's mean includes the new evaluations, so the pool and its best follow. It measures soon, after the
baseline, also when the campaign is stopped: not while it is paused with `--evals`, or held by its stop rule. A
commit that the daemon never measured, `skipped` or `recovered`, gets its first evaluations. If infrastructure
failures paused this candidate's task, the request also resumes evaluations of that task.

**Arguments**:

* `CANDIDATE_ID`: The candidate to measure again. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--repeats INT`: How many more evaluations. *[default: 3]*

#### piloop candidate note

```text
piloop candidate note [OPTIONS] CANDIDATE_ID REASON
```

Note why a candidate is worth starting from, with follow-up ideas about it.

The note is a comment on the candidate, signed as PILOOP_ACTOR. Each `--follow-up` is an open idea about the
candidate, whose run can start from it (`piloop idea claim <id> --base <candidate>`). An implementer files no
follow-ups.

**Arguments**:

* `CANDIDATE_ID`: The candidate. **[required]**
* `REASON`: Why the tree is worth starting from. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--follow-up STR..., --empty-follow-up`: An open idea about the candidate. Repeatable. *[default: ()]*
* `--track STR`: The track of the follow-ups. Default: the track of the candidate's run, else the first track.

#### piloop candidate exclude

```text
piloop candidate exclude --reason STR CANDIDATE_ID
```

Take a candidate out of the race for the best, such as one that gamed the evaluation.

Its scores stay in the pool, and a run may still start from it. The `candidate.excluded_reason` event records the
reason, signed as PILOOP_ACTOR. To take it back, set `excluded_reason` and `excluded_by` to NULL with `piloop sql`.

**Arguments**:

* `CANDIDATE_ID`: The candidate. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--reason STR`: Why it may not be best, with ids: what the number does not say. **[required]**

#### piloop cas serve

```text
piloop cas serve --listen STR
```

Serve the CAS of `[plugins.cas]` until Ctrl-C or SIGTERM. Each caller sends a worker's token.

Where the home's daemon does not hold its term (a standby under `[plugins.leader] provider = "bucket"`), it forwards

**Parameters**:

* `--listen STR`: Serve on HOST:PORT. An address that is not loopback needs TLS ([plugins.network]). **[required]**

### piloop doctor

```text
piloop doctor
```

Check the install without writing anything, and say how to fix each failure.

It notes a newer piloop at the source of the CLI, and checks that the daemon runs the installed code, the database

### piloop eval

List or cancel the evaluations that the daemon runs or that wait for a slot.

#### piloop eval queue

```text
piloop eval queue
```

List the running evaluations, then the waiting ones in the order in which they get a slot.

The last line counts them. A free slot of the executor goes to the campaign with the fewest running evaluations,
and in that campaign to its baseline first, then to the oldest request. A requested remeasure shows as waiting at once.
Each row names its evaluator. One that the campaign replaced since is `retired`: what runs finishes under it.

#### piloop eval cancel

```text
piloop eval cancel CANDIDATE_ID
```

Cancel the evaluation of a candidate: a waiting one never starts, and the daemon stops a running one.

It also cancels a requested remeasure. A cancel records no measurement, and it stands: the daemon does not evaluate
the candidate again under that evaluator, not to confirm it, not on the sealed split, and not after a restart. It is
evaluated again only when someone asks (`piloop candidate remeasure`). The candidate keeps its origin and its
scores. A baseline that never measured pauses its campaign's measurements: ask for it, then resume.

**Arguments**:

* `CANDIDATE_ID`: The candidate whose evaluation to cancel. **[required]**

### piloop events

```text
piloop events [OPTIONS]
```

Print the events after a cursor, oldest first, then the cursor to read after next.

The cursor moves over every event that the read examined, also those that --campaign and --kind leave out. Read a
snapshot first (`piloop status --json` or `piloop inbox --json`), then the events after its cursor: nothing is
missed in between. Save the cursor after you have handled the events, and read from it again after a disconnect.
An event can come twice then: skip an id that you have handled.

**Parameters**:

* `--after INT`: The cursor to read after: the `next` of the last read, or the `cursor` of a snapshot. *[default: 0]*
* `--campaign STR`: Only the events about rows of this campaign.
* `--kind STR..., --empty-kind`: Only events of this kind. Repeatable. *[default: ()]*
* `--limit INT`: The most events to print. *[default: 100]*
* `--follow`: Wait for an event when there is none yet, up to --timeout.
* `--timeout FLOAT`: With --follow: seconds to wait. *[default: 1800]*
* `--json`: Print one JSON document on stdout, for a program. Messages go to stderr.

### piloop idea

Add an idea to the board, claim or propose one for your run, and change an idea: its status, links or track.

#### piloop idea add

```text
piloop idea add [OPTIONS] REF TITLE
```

Add an idea to the board. It is open.

The `files` key of a role says whether its agents may add ideas. The orchestrator and humans may add ideas. The
idea is signed as PILOOP_ACTOR. The idea and its links are written together, so the daemon never sees the idea
without them.

**Arguments**:

* `REF`: The campaign name, its id, or a path inside its repo. **[required]**
* `TITLE`: A short title for the idea. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--hypothesis STR`: Why this should help, and by how much. Default: the title.
* `--plan STR`: The steps for the implementer.
* `--from STR`: What prompted the idea, with ids or a URL. Example: "note comment#881: …".
* `--priority INT`: From 0 (urgent) to 4 (someday). *[default: 2]*
* `--track STR`: The track it works on. Required when piloop.toml has more than one track.
* `--after INT..., --empty-after`: An idea id to wait for. Repeatable. A worker can claim the idea once all are done or dropped. *[default: ()]*

#### piloop idea claim

```text
piloop idea claim [OPTIONS] IDEA_ID
```

Claim an open idea for your run: you work on it until your run ends, and no other run takes it.

Only a worker claims, once per run. An idea that an `after` link holds waits until its prerequisites are done.
It prints the idea's hypothesis and its plan, which the board does not show, and, with several tracks, what the
idea's track asks of your run. With `--base`, your run starts from that candidate: your checkout moves to it.
Without, a run that the campaign's best (or trunk) passed since it was queued moves to the new one.

**Arguments**:

* `IDEA_ID`: The idea. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--base INT`: The candidate that your run starts from, instead of the best: any candidate of the campaign. Your checkout moves to it, and your submissions build on it.

#### piloop idea propose

```text
piloop idea propose [OPTIONS] TITLE
```

Add an idea of your own to the board and claim it for your run, in one step.

Only a worker proposes, instead of a claim. The idea is signed as PILOOP_ACTOR. With `--base`, your run starts
from that candidate: your checkout moves to it.

**Arguments**:

* `TITLE`: A short title for the idea. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--hypothesis STR`: Why this should help, and by how much. Default: the title.
* `--plan STR`: The steps you take.
* `--from STR`: What prompted the idea, with ids or a URL.
* `--track STR`: The track it works on. Required when piloop.toml has more than one track.
* `--base INT`: The candidate that your run starts from, instead of the best: any candidate of the campaign. Your checkout moves to it, and your submissions build on it.

#### piloop idea status

```text
piloop idea status --reason STR IDEA_ID STATUS
```

Set an idea's status, with a note on the idea that says why.

`open` puts it back on the board, and `dropped` takes it off, which frees the ideas after it. Only a run makes an
idea `done`. A claimed idea has an open run: it can only be dropped, and its run finishes.

**Arguments**:

* `IDEA_ID`: The idea. **[required]**
* `STATUS`: open or dropped. **[required]** *[choices: open, claimed, done, dropped]*

**Parameters**:

* `--reason STR`: Why, with ids. It goes into the event or a note on the idea. **[required]**

#### piloop idea link

```text
piloop idea link --reason STR IDEA_ID KIND TARGET_ID
```

Link an idea that exists to another idea of its campaign: it waits until the target is done.

The link is written in one transaction, so no worker claims the idea in between. A claimed idea takes no link: its
run started already. A link that closes a cycle is refused.

**Arguments**:

* `IDEA_ID`: The idea. **[required]**
* `KIND`: after: wait until the target is done or dropped. **[required]** *[choices: after]*
* `TARGET_ID`: The idea it links to. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--reason STR`: Why, with ids. It goes into the event or a note on the idea. **[required]**

#### piloop idea unlink

```text
piloop idea unlink --reason STR IDEA_ID KIND TARGET_ID
```

Delete an idea's link, for example an `after` link to a prerequisite that is still open, to free the idea.

**Arguments**:

* `IDEA_ID`: The idea. **[required]**
* `KIND`: after. **[required]** *[choices: after]*
* `TARGET_ID`: The idea it links to. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--reason STR`: Why, with ids. It goes into the event or a note on the idea. **[required]**

#### piloop idea track

```text
piloop idea track --reason STR IDEA_ID TRACK
```

Move an idea to another track of its campaign, with a note on the idea that says why.

Its runs are scored on that track's primary. A scheduled idea keeps its track: its run is scored on it.

**Arguments**:

* `IDEA_ID`: The idea. **[required]**
* `TRACK`: A track of piloop.toml. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--reason STR`: Why, with ids. It goes into the event or a note on the idea. **[required]**

### piloop inbox

#### piloop inbox show


#### piloop inbox wait

```text
piloop inbox wait [OPTIONS] [ARGS]
```

Wait until something needs the orchestrator, print it one line per item, then print the command to wait again.

These events end the wait: a new or edited unacknowledged inbox item that someone else wrote, a campaign that
the daemon stopped, and the daemon's score of a candidate. Your own decisions, as PILOOP_ACTOR, do not end it. At --timeout it returns with nothing.
Each return also names the escalations that still wait for you, unacknowledged and unanswered, with their age.
Set the timeout of your shell tool higher than --timeout. Without a campaign, it waits for every campaign.

**Arguments**:

* `REF`: The campaign name, its id, or a path inside its repo.

**Parameters**:

* `--timeout FLOAT`: Seconds to wait before it returns with nothing new. *[default: 1800]*
* `--after INT`: The event id to start after: the cursor that the last wait printed. Default: now.

#### piloop inbox ack

```text
piloop inbox ack [OPTIONS] [ARGS...]
```

Acknowledge items to say that you have seen them.

Nothing waits for an acknowledgement. To change a decision, write a `decision` comment on its target.

**Arguments**:

* `IDS`: The ids of the items.

**Parameters**:

* `--up-to INT`: Acknowledge every unacknowledged item up to this id instead. --campaign and --kind narrow it.
* `--campaign STR`: With --up-to: only the items of this campaign.
* `--kind COMMENTKIND..., --empty-kind`: With --up-to: only the items of this kind. Repeatable. *[default: ()]*

**Parameters**:

Answer an escalation with one of its choices: a `decision` reply signed as PILOOP_ACTOR, and an ack.

**Arguments**:

### piloop init

```text
piloop init [OPTIONS] [ARGS]
```

Make a directory a piloop campaign and register it.

It is safe to run again. When needed, it runs `git init` and makes a baseline commit. When there is no
piloop.toml, it writes piloop.toml, brief.md and evaluate.py from the template, and keeps the files that exist.
While evaluate.py is still the template stub, it stops and says what to edit. Then it starts the daemon, unless
one runs, registers the campaign with HEAD as its baseline candidate, and waits while the daemon measures it. A
baseline that fails to evaluate pauses the campaign, and init exits with why. Ctrl-C stops only the wait: the
campaign stays registered. For a campaign that is registered already, it reads piloop.toml again.

**Arguments**:

* `PATH`: The campaign directory. *[default: .]*

**Parameters**:

* `--example STR`: Write a bundled example instead of the blank template: enwik8, titanic-tracks, inference.
* `--name STR`: Register the campaign under this name, not the `name` of piloop.toml.

### piloop install-skills

```text
piloop install-skills
```

Install the orchestrator skills into ~/.agents/skills, and for every harness on PATH.

### piloop run

Act on one run.

#### piloop run cancel

```text
piloop run cancel [OPTIONS] RUN_ID
```

Cancel a queued or running run. The daemon acts at once.

A queued run never starts, and a run whose implementer fails to start is not launched again. A running implementer is ended
with every process that it started, at once or, with `--wrap-up`, after it was told to save its work and stop. The run
closes as `cancelled`, unless the implementer ran `piloop run end` before the daemon ended it: a later end is
refused. Its commits are scored, and its idea is open again: drop it to take it off the board.

**Arguments**:

* `RUN_ID`: The id of the run. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--wrap-up`: Tell the agent to save its work and stop first, and end it 10 minutes later.

#### piloop run extend

```text
piloop run extend --by TIMEDELTA RUN_ID
```

Give a running run's implementer more time.

Its timeout ends it `--by` later, also on a worker elsewhere, and a note tells it its new deadline. Extensions add up.

**Arguments**:

* `RUN_ID`: The id of the run. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--by TIMEDELTA`: How much more time, such as `1h` or `90m`. **[required]**

#### piloop run end

```text
piloop run end [OPTIONS] HOW
```

End your own run now, with every process that you started.

Optional: when your session exits, your run ends as `submitted` if it submitted a version, else as `gave_up`.
`submitted` settles your idea as done once your work is scored: in a campaign that scores what you submit, only
after a `piloop submit`. `gave_up` puts your idea back on the board, with your reason if you give one.

**Arguments**:

* `HOW`: submitted: your work is done. gave_up: you stop without a result. **[required]** *[choices: submitted, gave-up]*

**Parameters**:

* `--reason STR`: Why you give up, with ids.

### piloop serve

```text
piloop serve [OPTIONS]
```

Run the daemon, which launches the agents and reaps the agents that end.

Only one daemon runs per PILOOP_HOME. The file serve.pid holds its lock.

**Parameters**:

* `--check, --no-check`: Check that the daemon can start (its plugins, and what config.toml binds), and start nothing.

### piloop service-unit

### piloop sql

```text
piloop sql [OPTIONS] STATEMENT
```

Run one SQL statement on the piloop database, with the campaign's own tables attached as the schema `team`.

These are denied: schema changes outside `team`, ATTACH, transactions, and writes to `alembic_version` and
`session_actor`. In `team`, CREATE and DROP of a TABLE, an INDEX or a VIEW, and ALTER TABLE, are allowed. Only
the PRAGMAs that describe the schema are allowed. A statement runs for at most 5 s and `team` grows to at most
256 MiB (`[sql]` in config.toml). `actor()` returns whom the statement signs as. A query prints at most 500 rows.
With `-`, the statement is read from stdin: piped in from a quoted heredoc (`<<'EOF'`), an agent's text reaches
the database as written, where a double-quoted argument would let the shell run what is in backticks or `$(…)`
first.

**Arguments**:

* `STATEMENT`: One SQL statement, or - to read it from stdin. **[required]**

**Parameters**:

* `--campaign STR`: The campaign whose team tables are attached: its name or id. By default the only campaign that is not retired, else the one cwd is in. An agent's are always its own campaign's.

### piloop upgrade

```text

```

Upgrade the CLI, the skills and the database to the latest version, and restart a running daemon.
