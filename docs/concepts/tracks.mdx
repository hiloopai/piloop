<Note>Experimental: tracks.</Note>

Sometimes you want to improve two things about the same code, for example the accuracy of a model and its cost to
run. Run them as **tracks** of one project, not as two projects. Each track has its own primary metric. A keep on one
track must hold the primary of every other track within a margin.

## The example

`piloop init --example titanic-tracks` creates a project with two tracks on Kaggle's Titanic data. Workers edit
`solve.py`, which trains a model and predicts survival. One evaluate command prints two metrics:

- `accuracy`: the accuracy on a fixed holdout. Higher is better.
- `cost_seconds`: the CPU seconds that `solve.py` takes to train and predict. Lower is better.

```toml
[[metrics]]
name = "accuracy"
direction = "higher"
role = "primary"
track = "accuracy"
margin = 0.02          # what a cost keep may give up per step
floor = 0.74           # never below, whatever the bar

[[metrics]]
name = "cost_seconds"
direction = "lower"
role = "primary"
track = "cost"
margin = 0.3           # what an accuracy keep may add per step
floor = 2.0            # an accuracy keep may not push the cost past this

[tracks.accuracy]
share = 0.75           # of [roles.worker] max_concurrent
addendum = "briefs/accuracy.md"

[tracks.cost]
share = 0.25
addendum = "briefs/cost.md"
```

## How tracks work

- Each primary metric names its `track`. With several primaries, each one needs a `margin` and may have a `floor`.
- Each track has a `[tracks.<name>]` table. `share` is the part of the worker slots that the track gets. The shares
  sum to 1. `addendum` is a file that is added to the brief of the track's runs.
- Every idea names its track: `piloop idea add <project> "<title>" --track cost`. With several tracks, piloop refuses
  an idea without one.
- A candidate is judged on the track of its idea. The primary of that track must beat the bar. The primary of every
  other track is one more guardrail, with its own margin and floor.
- All tracks share one trunk and one head. A keep on any track moves the head.

A project with one primary metric is one track, named after the metric. It needs no `[tracks]` table.

## What you see

- `piloop status` and `piloop watch` show the head with the primary of every track. Then one line for each track: its
  primary at the head and at the start, the distance to its floor, its keeps, and its share against its running runs.
- A free worker slot goes to the track that is furthest below its share and has a ready idea. A track with no ready
  idea gives its slot to the others. A share of 0 stops a track.
- A keep on one track that makes the other track's primary worse than its margin is `interesting`. For example:
  `guardrails: cost_seconds 0.49 vs 0.09 (+0.4; …) beyond its margin 0.3`.
- A keep on one track that pushes the other track's primary past its floor is a `discard`. For example:
  `floor: cost_seconds 2.3 past its floor 2`.
- The research agent fills the backlog of each track by its share, and names the tracks that are short.

## What is the same for every track

The evaluate command, the `[stop]` conditions, the `[spend]` limit and the watchdog's timer are for the whole
project. `[stop] target` is not allowed with several tracks.
