---
title: Candidates, the head and the verdict
description: How piloop decides whether a commit is kept.
---

piloop measures each commit that an implementer submits and compares it with the head, the best result so far. This
is the verdict on the first submission of the enwik8 example:

```text
candidate#2 keep: against candidate#1: bytes 2720458 vs 3688775 (+968317; noise 0 over 1 evaluations)
```

It came from these metrics in `piloop.toml`:

```toml
[[metrics]]
name = "bytes"
direction = "lower"
role = "primary"

[[metrics]]
name = "seconds"
direction = "lower"
role = "guardrail"
margin = 60       # may get 60 s worse for each keep
floor = 120       # and never worse than 120 s
repeats = 3       # a time: measure it 3 times next to the bar
```

## The words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **candidate** | One state of the code, identified by its git tree. Each commit of a run is one. A commit back to a known tree is that candidate again, and is not measured again. |
| **head** | The latest kept candidate on the trunk. New runs start from it. |
| **bar** | What a run's commits must beat: the run's starting point, then each keep that the run makes. |
| **verdict** | The daemon's judgement of a measured candidate. Until it has one, the candidate is **pending**. |

## The verdicts

| Verdict | When |
|---|---|
| `keep` | The primary beat the bar by more than the noise, held on the sealed split, and every guardrail held. On the trunk, it is the new head. |
| `interesting` | A guardrail got worse by more than its `margin` or `fraction`. |
| `discard` | The primary did not beat the bar by more than the noise, lost on the sealed split, a guardrail went past its floor, or its climb [stopped at a rung](/concepts/campaigns#the-evaluation-ladder). |
| `crash` | No successful measurement: the evaluate command failed, timed out, or missed a metric. |

The rule, `noise`, in order:

1. No value for the primary: `discard` when its climb stopped at a rung, else `crash`.
2. A guardrail past its `floor`: `discard`.
3. The bar has no score under the current evaluator: `keep`.
4. The gain over the bar is at most `m·σ/√n`: `discard`.
5. The gain does not hold on the [sealed split](#the-sealed-split): `discard`.
6. A guardrail worse than the bar by more than its `margin`, or than its `fraction` of the bar's value: `interesting`.
7. Otherwise: `keep`.

## Submissions

An implementer runs `piloop submit` in its checkout. piloop scores that commit with the evaluate command at once, judges
it against the head, and prints the verdict and the score:

```text
keep 2720458
```

- A run has `[evaluator] submissions` of them, 3 by default. A commit that is not submitted is never scored: at the
  end of the run it is `skipped`.
- A submission that beats only its parent is recorded, nothing more. The `candidate.judged` event has the gain over
  the parent (`parent_delta`) and over the head (`best_delta`).
- The implementer gets the verdict and the score, never the reason, the feedback or a per-unit result.
- A run that claimed no idea submits nothing.

`[evaluator] scoring = "run_end"` scores every commit after the run ends instead, against the run's starting point.

## The noise

The same version can score differently from one evaluation to the next. piloop measures how much, then keeps only a
gain bigger than that.

- **`σ`, the noise:** the standard deviation of the baseline's score over its first `[evaluator] noise_runs`
  successful evaluations, 3 by default, under each evaluator.
- **The threshold:** a candidate's mean gain over the bar must exceed `m·σ/√n`. `n` is how many times the candidate
  was evaluated, and `m` is 2 by default (`[plugins.verdict_rule] m`). A mean of `n` evaluations is `√n` times less
  noisy than one, so the threshold drops as the evidence grows.
- **One more evaluation when it is close:** a first gain above 0 but at most `m·σ` runs once more. The mean of the two
  then has to beat `m·σ/√2`.

With `σ = 2` and `m = 2`, one evaluation needs a gain above 4; two need a mean gain above 2.83. A deterministic score
has `σ = 0`: any gain keeps. When the baseline's evaluations crash, `σ` is unknown: a gain is `interesting`, never a
`keep`, and piloop escalates once.

## The sealed split

`[evaluator] sealed` is a second command that scores a version on data that no implementer can read: keep its data
and its scorer in the home or on the evaluator's machines, outside the repo. piloop runs it only on a submission that
would become the new head, and on that head once. The submission is kept only when it beats the head there too.

```toml
[evaluator]
sealed = ["python3", "/srv/sealed/score.py"]
```

The daemon never judges three kinds of candidate:

| `candidates.origin` | What it is |
|---|---|
| `reference` | A starting point from outside the campaign: `piloop candidate import`. Once measured, one that builds on the head can be kept by hand, and is the head then. |
| `skipped` | A commit with a `Piloop-Eval: skip` trailer. |
| `recovered` | What an implementer left uncommitted when it stopped. The next attempt at the idea starts from it. |

## The bootstrap rule

`[plugins.verdict_rule] provider = "bootstrap"` keeps a candidate when its primary beats the bar with confidence. A
gain that is not significant is `interesting`. How it compares a metric depends on what the evaluate command gives it:

| The evaluate command gives | piloop compares |
|---|---|
| [Per-unit results](/concepts/campaigns#per-unit-results) | The units that both sides report, with a paired bootstrap: 4000 resamples, and the worst case at the `confidence` level, 0.95 by default. |
| A metric with `repeats` | `repeats` pairs, each one run of the candidate and one of the bar back to back on one worker, with the same bootstrap. |
| Neither | The aggregates. Any gain counts, and the reason says `aggregate`. Right for a deterministic metric, such as a size. |

So a gain from three lucky units has the spread of three units. The bootstrap has a fixed seed: the same numbers
always give the same verdict.

## When the head moves during a run

Two runs can start from the same head. When the first keeps, the head moves. A later keep of the second is then
`interesting`, with the cause `moved`. The daemon merges it onto the new head, and measures and judges the merge
there. A merge that conflicts stays `interesting`, and its reason names the paths: piloop files no idea for it.

A commit that already has the head, such as a merge of it, is judged against the head, not against the run's bar.

A verdict set by hand can replace the head. Then piloop cancels the open runs that started from the old head, and
their ideas are open again, for a run from the new head.

## Act on a candidate

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `piloop candidate remeasure 42` | Measure it again, 3 times next to its bar. A `crash`, `discard` or `interesting` that the daemon gave is judged again. A keep stays. |
| `piloop candidate verdict 42 interesting --reason "lucky seed"` | Override the verdict. The daemon never overwrites a verdict that it did not set. |
| `piloop candidate note 42 "<why>" --follow-up "<idea>"` | Keep a regressed result in view: it becomes `interesting`, with the cause `note`, and each follow-up is an open idea about it. Who set the verdict stays, unless the note turns a `discard` into a lead. The daemon never judges a noted candidate again. |
| `piloop measurement add 42 --report report.json` | Record an evaluation that you ran, such as `{"metrics": {"accuracy": 0.853}, "seconds": 600}`. |
| `piloop eval queue`, `piloop eval cancel 42` | List the evaluations, or cancel one. A cancel is no verdict: a run's candidate is `skipped`. |

```bash
piloop candidate remeasure 1
```

```text
candidate#1 keep: the daemon measures it 3 times alone; its verdict stays
```

An implementer notes its own commit with a `Piloop-Note: <why>` trailer. Each worker runs at most `evaluations`
evaluate commands at a time, a key of its [pool](/guides/workers#capacity). A free slot goes to the campaign with the
fewest running evaluations, then to its head. To stop an evaluation, use `piloop eval cancel`, not `pkill`: the daemon
runs a killed evaluation once more.

`noise` and `bootstrap` are the built-in verdict rules. A plugin can replace them: see
[Verdict rules](/plugins/rules#verdict-rules).
