piloop scores each commit that an implementer submits into the campaign's **pool**, and nothing leaves it. The
**best** is the candidate with the best score in the pool. This is what the implementer of the first run of the enwik8
example hears:

```text
[piloop] candidate#2 (3f2c9a1b7d04): bytes 2720458 over 1 eval: the best of the bytes track now, rank 1 of 2; vs parent +968317
```

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **candidate** | One state of the code, identified by its git tree. Each submitted commit is one. |
| **baseline** | The first candidate: the repo's `HEAD` when you registered the campaign. |
| **confirmed** | A candidate with `[evaluator] noise_runs` successful evaluations, 3 by default. |
| **best** | The candidate with the best mean of the primary among the baseline and the confirmed candidates that the [best rule](#the-best) allows. |
| **line** | A candidate's chain of parents. |

## Scoring

1. The daemon evaluates a submission once. A [ladder](/concepts/campaigns#the-evaluation-ladder) can stop that
   evaluation at a rung.
2. When the score beats the best's mean, the daemon evaluates the submission until it is confirmed. It also scores it
   on the [sealed split](#the-sealed-split), if the campaign has one.
3. The daemon tells the implementer where the submission stands.

The daemon evaluates the baseline `noise_runs` times before runs start. When those evaluations agree, the evaluator is
deterministic, and one evaluation confirms. Otherwise the standard deviation of the best's evaluations is the noise,
**σ**.

## The best

The best is a query of the pool: nothing stores it, and the order of the evaluations never decides it. When a
remeasure changes a mean, the best follows.

The **best rule** says whether a confirmed candidate may be the best. The default rule, `baseline`, refuses a candidate
when:

1. A primary or a guardrail is past its `floor`.
2. A guardrail is worse than the baseline's by more than its `margin` or `fraction`.
3. The campaign has a sealed split, and the candidate does not beat the baseline there.

A plugin can replace the rule: see [Best rules](/plugins/rules#best-rules).

## The sealed split

`[evaluator] sealed` is a second command that scores a version on data that no implementer can read. Keep its data and
its scorer outside the repo:

```toml
[evaluator]
sealed = ["python3", "/srv/sealed/score.py"]
```

piloop runs it on the baseline and on each candidate that would beat the best. Its score is the primary of the first
track. A result that piloop refuses is a fault of the setup: piloop escalates, and runs it again later. Fix the command
and run `piloop campaign sync`.

## What a worker hears

`piloop submit` returns at once, and the implementer works on:

```text
candidate#12 is queued: where it stands comes as a [piloop] message
```

The message gives the score, the rank among the candidates that may be best, and the gains:

```text
[piloop] candidate#14 (9b04e1c27a3f): bytes 2370112 over 1 eval: rank 2 of 5; vs parent -8219, vs best candidate#12 -8219
```

- `vs parent`, `vs your line's best` and `vs best` are the gains. A positive gain is better.
- `(within noise)` marks a gain within 2 σ.
- `it may not be best: …` gives the best rule's reason.
- Every harness reads the message at its next `piloop` command, which prints it first. Claude Code also reads it after
  its next tool call.

A run submits as many commits as it likes, unless `[evaluator] submissions` caps it. A commit that is not submitted is
never scored. `[evaluator] scoring = "run_end"` scores every commit after the run instead.

## Lines and bases

A run starts from the best. An implementer can start from another candidate when it claims its idea:

```bash
piloop idea claim 7 --base 14
```

The checkout moves to that candidate, and the run's submissions are its children. The brief names up to 3 other
candidates worth branching from: the best of each other line.

`piloop candidate import enwik8 <commit>` makes any commit a **reference** candidate, such as a branch. It gets one
evaluation. To confirm it, remeasure it.

## Act on a candidate

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `piloop candidate show 42` | Print where it stands, its metrics beside its parent's, and its rungs. |
| `piloop candidate remeasure 42` | Evaluate it 3 more times. Its mean includes the new evaluations. |
| `piloop candidate exclude 42 --reason "<why>"` | Take it out of the race for the best, such as one that gamed the evaluation. A run may still start from it. |
| `piloop candidate note 42 "<why>" --follow-up "<idea>"` | Note why it is worth starting from. Each follow-up is an open idea. |
| `piloop measurement add 42 --report report.json` | Record an evaluation that you ran yourself. |
| `piloop eval queue`, `piloop eval cancel 42` | List the evaluations, or cancel one. |

To stop an evaluation, use `piloop eval cancel`, not `pkill`: the daemon runs a killed evaluation once more. Each
worker runs at most `evaluations` evaluate commands at a time ([capacity](/guides/workers#capacity)).

## The result

The campaign's result is the best of each track. `piloop status` shows it with its gain over the baseline:

```text
## Campaign

best: candidate#12 · bytes: 2361893 · bytes vs baseline: 1326882 · may be best on bytes: 5 · candidates: 15

## Trajectory

- Baseline candidate#1, best candidate#12: bytes 3688775 → 2361893 (-1326882, -36.0%); 3 new bests.
```

A **new best** is a score that made its candidate the best when the daemon scored it. `piloop history` shows the
metrics of the best beside the baseline's.

## Tracks

A **track** is one objective of a campaign that has several, such as the accuracy of a model and its cost to run.
Tracks are experimental. `piloop init --example titanic-tracks` makes a campaign with two:

```toml
[[metrics]]
name = "accuracy"
direction = "higher"
role = "primary"
track = "accuracy"
margin = 0.02          # what the best of cost may lose against the baseline
floor = 0.74           # and never below this

[[metrics]]
name = "cost_seconds"
direction = "lower"
role = "primary"
track = "cost"
margin = 0.3           # what the best of accuracy may add to the baseline
floor = 2.0            # and never past this

[tracks.accuracy]
addendum = "briefs/accuracy.md"
```

- Each idea names its track: `piloop idea add titanic-tracks "Add a family-size feature" --track accuracy`.
- Each track has its own best. On each track, the other tracks' primaries are guardrails against the baseline, with
  their own `margin` and `floor`.
- A candidate is scored on its idea's track. A candidate without an idea, such as the baseline, is on the first track.
- A run starts from the best of the first track, or from the candidate that `--base` names.
- When a worker claims an idea, it learns its track and gets the track's `addendum` file.
