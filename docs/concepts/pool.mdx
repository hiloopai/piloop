piloop scores each commit that an implementer submits into the campaign's **pool**, and nothing leaves it. The
**best** is the candidate with the best score in the pool. This is what the implementer of the first run of the enwik8
example hears:

```text
[piloop] candidate#2 (3f2c9a1b7d04): bytes 2720458 over 1 eval: the best of the bytes track now, rank 1 of 2; vs parent +968317
```

It came from these metrics in `piloop.toml`:

```toml
[[metrics]]
name = "bytes"
direction = "lower"
role = "primary"

[[metrics]]
name = "seconds"
direction = "lower"
role = "guardrail"
margin = 60       # may get 60 s worse than the baseline
floor = 120       # and never worse than 120 s
```

## The words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **candidate** | One state of the code, identified by its git tree. Each submitted commit of a run is one. A commit back to a known tree is that candidate again. |
| **baseline** | The first candidate: the repo's HEAD when you registered the campaign. It may always be the best. |
| **confirmed** | A candidate with `[evaluator] noise_runs` successful evaluations, 3 by default. Only a confirmed candidate may be the best. |
| **best** | The candidate with the best mean of the primary among the baseline and the confirmed candidates that the [best rule](#the-best) allows. |
| **line** | A candidate's chain of parents. |

## Scoring

1. The daemon evaluates a submission once. With a [ladder](/concepts/campaigns#the-evaluation-ladder), that
   evaluation can stop at a rung, against the best's values there.
2. When the score beats the best's mean, the daemon evaluates the submission again until it is confirmed. At the same
   time, it scores the submission on the [sealed split](#the-sealed-split), if the campaign has one.
3. The daemon tells the implementer where the submission stands.

The daemon evaluates the baseline `noise_runs` times, and runs wait for its first evaluation. When its evaluations agree
on each primary, the evaluator is deterministic, and one evaluation confirms. Otherwise the standard deviation of the
best's evaluations is the noise, **σ**, that a message names.

## The best

The best is a query of the pool, and nothing stores it. So the order of the evaluations never decides it. When a
remeasure changes a mean, the best follows.

The **best rule** says whether a confirmed candidate may be the best. The default rule, `baseline`, refuses a candidate
when:

1. A primary or a guardrail is past its `floor`.
2. A guardrail is worse than the baseline's by more than its `margin`, or than its `fraction` of the baseline's value.
3. The campaign has a sealed split, and the candidate does not beat the baseline there.

A plugin can replace the rule: see [Best rules](/plugins/rules#best-rules). Each track has its own best:
see [Tracks](/concepts/tracks).

## The sealed split

`[evaluator] sealed` is a second command that scores a version on data that no implementer can read. Keep its data
and its scorer in the home or on the evaluator's machines, outside the repo.

```toml
[evaluator]
sealed = ["python3", "/srv/sealed/score.py"]
```

piloop runs it on the baseline and on each candidate that it confirms. Its score is the primary of the first track.

A result of the sealed command that piloop refuses, such as no `score`, is a fault of the setup: no implementer can
change that command. The evaluation runs again after `[evaluation] infra_retry`, and piloop escalates once. Fix the
command and run `piloop campaign sync`: the next confirmation uses the new one. `{"status": "infra_error"}` from the
command is a fault of the machine, and the evaluation runs again later too.

## What a worker hears

An implementer runs `piloop submit` in its checkout. It returns at once, and the implementer works on:

```text
candidate#12 is queued: where it stands comes as a [piloop] message
```

The message gives the score, the place among the candidates that may be best, and the gains:

```text
[piloop] candidate#14 (9b04e1c27a3f): bytes 2370112 over 1 eval: rank 2 of 5; vs parent -8219, vs best candidate#12 -8219
```

- `vs parent` is the gain over its parent, `vs your line's best` over the best of its line, and `vs best` over the
  best. A positive gain is better.
- `(within noise)` marks a gain within 2 standard deviations of the difference, with `(σ …)` at the end.
- `it may not be best: …` gives the reason of the best rule. `excluded (…)` gives the reason of an
  [exclusion](#act-on-a-candidate).
- A harness that reads messages only between turns gets no message. The `candidate.scored` event has the same line
  (`told`).

A run submits as many commits as it likes, unless `[evaluator] submissions` caps it. A commit that is not submitted is
never scored: at the end of the run it is `skipped`. A resubmit of a tree that the run submitted is the same
submission. When all its evaluations failed, such as on a timeout under load, piloop evaluates it once more. A tree
that is another candidate already is refused. A run that claimed no idea submits nothing.

`[evaluator] scoring = "run_end"` scores every commit after the run ends instead.

## Lines and bases

A run starts from the best. An implementer can start from another candidate when it claims its idea:

```bash
piloop idea claim 7 --base 14
```

The checkout moves to that candidate, and the run's submissions are children of it. The brief names up to 3 other
candidates worth branching from: the best of each line apart from the best's.

These candidates never enter the pool:

| `candidates.origin` | What it is |
|---|---|
| `skipped` | A commit with a `Piloop-Eval: skip` trailer, or one that was not submitted. |
| `recovered` | What an implementer left uncommitted when it stopped. The next attempt at the idea starts from it. |

A `reference` from `piloop candidate import` gets one evaluation. To confirm it, remeasure it.

## Act on a candidate

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `piloop candidate show 42` | Print where it stands, its metrics beside its parent's, and its rungs. |
| `piloop candidate remeasure 42` | Evaluate it 3 more times. Its mean includes the new evaluations. |
| `piloop candidate exclude 42 --reason "<why>"` | Take it out of the race for the best, such as one that gamed the evaluation. Its scores stay, and a run may still start from it. |
| `piloop candidate note 42 "<why>" --follow-up "<idea>"` | Note why it is worth starting from. Each follow-up is an open idea about it. |
| `piloop measurement add 42 --report report.json` | Record an evaluation that you ran, such as `{"metrics": {"bytes": 2720458}, "seconds": 600}`. |
| `piloop eval queue`, `piloop eval cancel 42` | List the evaluations, or cancel one. A run's cancelled candidate is `skipped`. |

```bash
piloop candidate remeasure 14
```

```text
candidate#14: the daemon evaluates it 3 times
```

To take an exclusion back, set `excluded_reason` and `excluded_by` to NULL with `piloop sql`. Each worker runs at most
`evaluations` evaluate commands at a time, a key of its [worker pool](/guides/workers#capacity). A free slot goes to the
campaign with the fewest running evaluations, then to its baseline, then to the oldest request. To stop an
evaluation, use `piloop eval cancel`, not `pkill`: the daemon runs a killed evaluation once more.

## The result

The campaign's result is the best of each track. `piloop status` shows it with its gain over the baseline and the
latest new bests:

```text
## Campaign

best: candidate#12 · bytes: 2361893 · bytes vs baseline: 1326882 · may be best on bytes: 5 · candidates: 15

## Trajectory

- Baseline candidate#1, best candidate#12: bytes 3688775 → 2361893 (-1326882, -36.0%); 3 new bests.
```

A **new best** is a score that made its candidate the best when the daemon scored it. The Trajectory section also lists
the other lines worth branching from, and `piloop history` the metrics of the best beside the baseline's.
