The **orchestrator** is the coding agent that you talk to. It runs the campaign for you: it answers what the other
agents escalate, manages the ideas and reports. Open your agent in the campaign repo and ask it to "orchestrate the
piloop campaign". It follows the `piloop-operate` skill, and loops on one command:

```bash
PILOOP_ACTOR=orchestrator piloop inbox wait enwik8 --timeout 1800
```

```text
candidate#2 keep: against candidate#1: bytes 2720458 vs 3688775 (-968317; 95% worst case -968317 over 3 pairs); guardrails…
campaign enwik8 stopped: max_runs: 1 runs
next: piloop inbox wait enwik8 --after 17
```

The wait returns on a new inbox item, verdict, anomaly or stop, or at the timeout. Its last line is the next
wait, with a cursor, so nothing is lost between two waits. The orchestrator uses no tokens while it waits. The daemon
runs without one. Run one orchestrator for each campaign: two would answer an escalation twice.

## Let piloop launch it

With the `news` trigger on `[plugins.role.orchestrator]`, the daemon launches the orchestrator when news arrives, and nobody
needs to keep a session open:

```toml
[plugins.role.orchestrator]
triggers = ["news"]
```

- A message, an escalation or the stop launches it within `quiet_seconds` (30). Other news, such as a verdict, launches
  it at most once in `routine_every` seconds (900).
- While an orchestrator works, new news goes to it as a note, at once. Claude Code reads it after its current tool
  call. Codex and pi read it when their turn ends.
- `piloop agent cancel` on an orchestrator drops the news it was launched for: no other one starts for it. News that
  arrives after the cancel launches the next one.
- While the campaign is paused or held, only a message or an escalation launches it.
- Each orchestrator resumes the session of the one before, so it remembers what it decided. When it cannot, it starts
  from its whole brief, and `agent_session.not_resumed` says why.
- Its writes sign as `orchestrator#<id>`. The inbox treats every orchestrator, launched or not, as one: none waits for
  what another wrote.

Do not run `piloop-operate` on a campaign whose orchestrator piloop launches. Write to it with `piloop message`.

## The inbox

The **inbox** is every comment that has a priority: 0 and 1 are urgent, 2 to 4 can wait.

```bash
piloop inbox
```

## Comments

Agents and people talk through **comments** on a campaign, an idea, a run, a candidate or another comment. A comment on
an idea goes into the brief of its next run.

| Kind | Use |
|---|---|
| `note` | Context, or a short remark. |
| `finding` | What an agent tried and learned. |
| `decision` | A decision, with the reason and how to revert it. |
| `escalation` | A question that needs a decision, filed with `piloop escalate`. It always has a priority. |
| `message` | Someone's word to the orchestrator. |

## Escalations

An agent asks for a decision with `piloop escalate`, and its form says it in plain words:

```bash
piloop escalate campaign#1 --priority 1 <<'JSON'
{"ask": "Do you approve a longer timeout?",
 "why": "Each run takes 3 hours. The timeout is 1 hour.",
 "options": [{"choice": "Raise it to 4 hours.", "result": "Each run costs more."},
             {"choice": "Keep it.", "result": "The idea stops."}],
 "recommend": 0,
 "details": "run#41 timed out."}
JSON
piloop inbox show 1
```

```text
Do you approve a longer timeout?

Each run takes 3 hours. The timeout is 1 hour.

A. Raise it to 4 hours. (recommended) → Each run costs more.
B. Keep it. → The idea stops.
Answer: `piloop inbox choose 1 <A|B>`

Details:
run#41 timed out.
```

`piloop inbox choose 1 A` answers it: a decision signed as you, and an acknowledgement.

- An agent's escalation goes to the orchestrator first. The orchestrator answers it, gives its duplicates the same
  answer, or files one escalation of its own for you. A [notifier](/guides/notifications) sends it to you only when
  nobody has acknowledged it 30 minutes after it was filed.
- The orchestrator's escalations, and the daemon's, reach you at once.
- The form, its limits and that wait are the campaign's `[plugins.escalation]`. `structured`, built in, is the
  default. The [configuration reference](/reference/configuration#plugins) lists its keys.

## Decisions

The orchestrator decides what comes up, so work never waits for you. It records each decision that it takes for you as
a `decision` in your inbox:

```text
[<category>, reversible|irreversible] <what it decided, in one line>
Why: <the evidence, with ids>
Changed: <the rows, files, settings or commits>
Revert: <the command or edit that undoes it>
```

A change to a goal, a metric, a guardrail, the evaluate command or a stop condition has priority 0. A decision that
cannot be undone, and can wait, it leaves to you as an escalation. To revise a decision, reply to it: in
`piloop watch`, press `r`. The orchestrator applies your reply first.

## Several campaigns

One daemon serves every campaign of a `PILOOP_HOME`. To improve two repos, set up a campaign in each. Two goals for the
same code, such as accuracy and cost, are [tracks](/concepts/tracks) of one campaign. One agent can orchestrate every
campaign: ask it to "orchestrate all my piloop campaigns".
